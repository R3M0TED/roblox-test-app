# Base Game Idea

## One-line pitch

A 1–6 player cooperative supermarket night-shift simulator where employees complete ordinary retail tasks while surviving increasingly disturbing paranormal events inside a supermarket that never seems to close.

## Genre / inspiration

- Cooperative horror
- Job simulator
- Survival
- Supermarket simulation
- Atmospheric psychological horror
- Inspired by multiplayer survival horror and retail simulation games.

The game should balance supermarket simulation and horror survival approximately 50/50.

The defining feature is gradual horror escalation: the game initially feels like a normal supermarket job before becoming increasingly unsettling and dangerous.

## Core gameplay loop

Players work overnight shifts at a supermarket.

Each shift involves two interconnected gameplay experiences:

**Supermarket simulation:**
- Restock shelves.
- Scan products at checkout.
- Serve customers.
- Clean spills and rubbish.
- Handle deliveries.
- Organise inventory.
- Complete assigned objectives.

**Horror survival:**
- Investigate strange occurrences.
- Identify supernatural entities.
- Avoid or escape threats.
- Hide when necessary.
- Monitor security cameras.
- Help teammates survive.
- Complete the shift without being caught.

Each shift has several phases:

1. Opening: Players clock in and receive objectives.
2. Early shift: Normal supermarket work.
3. Mid shift: Strange events begin occurring.
4. Late shift: Entities become increasingly active.
5. Closing: Complete remaining objectives and survive until the shift ends.

Suggested initial shift duration: 12–15 minutes.

Players earn money and experience for completing tasks and surviving shifts.

## Players

- Cooperative multiplayer.
- 1–6 players per server.
- Solo play should remain possible.
- Players can freely choose which supermarket tasks to complete.
- Teamwork is encouraged but not mandatory.
- Players can communicate and coordinate during supernatural encounters.
- Some tasks and events benefit from multiple employees.

Possible responsibilities include:
- Cashier.
- Stocker.
- Cleaner.
- Security.
- Manager.

These are informal responsibilities rather than permanent character classes.

## Progression / rewards

Players earn money and experience by completing tasks, objectives and shifts.

Money can be spent on:
- Equipment upgrades.
- Flashlight upgrades and skins.
- Employee outfits.
- Cosmetic items.
- Emotes.
- Additional equipment.

Experience can unlock:
- New shifts.
- New supermarket locations.
- Additional equipment.
- Employee titles.
- New challenges.

### Shift progression

- Night 1: Orientation and basic supermarket gameplay.
- Night 2: First entity introduction.
- Night 3: Security systems and restricted areas.
- Night 4: Multiple entities and more dangerous encounters.
- Night 5: Major story reveal and challenging survival scenarios.
- Night 6+: Additional threats, mechanics and locations.

Progression should unlock content rather than make players powerful enough to ignore the horror.

## Look and feel

- 3D Roblox environment.
- Atmospheric, realistic-inspired supermarket design.
- Initially bright, welcoming and ordinary.
- Gradually becomes darker, quieter and more unsettling.
- Fluorescent lights, long aisles, CCTV cameras, warehouses and storage rooms.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Horror should rely on atmosphere, tension and unexpected encounters rather than constant jumpscares.

### Audio

Normal gameplay:
- Supermarket background music.
- Checkout sounds.
- Shopping trolley noises.
- Refrigerators humming.
- PA announcements.
- Footsteps and employee chatter.

Horror gameplay:
- Distorted announcements.
- Distant footsteps.
- Unexplained noises.
- Flickering light sounds.
- Entity-specific audio cues.
- Periods of complete silence.

## Entity system

The game should contain multiple supernatural entities with distinct behaviours.

Players must learn how each entity behaves and how to survive its encounters.

Initial entity concepts:

### The Customer
- Appears to be an ordinary customer.
- Follows players around the supermarket.
- Watches employees from a distance.
- Disappears when approached.
- Becomes aggressive under specific conditions.
- Primarily stalks isolated employees.

### The Night Cleaner
- A mysterious figure with a cleaning cart.
- Patrols specific supermarket sections.
- Reacts to noise.
- Becomes hostile when players enter restricted territory.
- Can block important routes.

### The Observer
- Appears through security camera feeds.
- Moves between camera locations.
- Can interfere with CCTV.
- Becomes more dangerous when watched for too long.

### The Impostor
- Mimics customers or employees.
- Can give misleading instructions.
- Behaves incorrectly while performing ordinary tasks.
- Can appear in areas where nobody should be.

Only one fully implemented entity is required for the first playable version. Other entities are future content.

Entities should have distinct detection, movement, attack and counterplay behaviours.

## Random events

Random events make shifts less predictable.

Initial event ideas:
- Power outages.
- False fire alarms.
- Missing customers.
- Stockroom noises.
- CCTV interference.
- Automatic doors opening unexpectedly.
- Strange deliveries.
- Distorted PA announcements.
- Unexplained objects moving.
- Lights turning off in individual sections.

Events should vary in severity. Not every strange event should introduce an entity.

## Survival mechanics

- Health system.
- Sprinting and stamina.
- Flashlights.
- Hiding locations.
- Noise-based detection.
- Downed player state.
- Teammate revival.
- Security cameras.

Players who are attacked enter a downed state. Teammates can revive them within a limited time.

Players who are not revived become spectators until the shift ends.

The team can still complete a shift without every employee surviving.

Avoid unnecessary survival systems such as hunger and thirst in the initial version.

## Map / environment

The first map should be one medium-sized supermarket.

Required areas:
- Main supermarket aisles.
- Checkout area.
- Warehouse.
- Staff room.
- Security office.
- Freezer section.
- Toilets.
- Loading bay.
- Car park.

Each area should have a gameplay purpose.

The store should initially be well-lit and welcoming. As shifts progress, environmental changes, lighting and sound should increase tension.

## Must-haves for the first playable version

### Environment
- One complete supermarket map.
- Main aisles.
- Checkout area.
- Warehouse.
- Staff room.
- Security office.
- Basic atmospheric lighting.

### Supermarket simulation
- Shelf restocking.
- Checkout mechanic.
- Cleaning mechanic.
- Basic customer NPCs.
- Task and objective system.
- Basic inventory system.

### Horror
- One fully functioning supernatural entity.
- Three to five random paranormal events.
- Entity detection and attack behaviour.
- Basic hiding mechanic.
- Flashlight.
- Basic horror audio and lighting effects.

### Multiplayer
- Support for 1–6 players.
- Shared shift objectives.
- Individual task tracking.
- Downed and revive system.
- Basic player communication through Roblox chat.

### Progression
- Shift timer.
- Shift completion and failure conditions.
- Money rewards.
- Basic equipment shop.
- Subsequent shift unlocks.

### General
- Lobby.
- Shift start system.
- Basic UI.
- Reliable multiplayer task handling.
- Complete playable shift from beginning to end.

## Later / nice-to-haves

- Additional supernatural entities.
- More supermarket locations.
- More departments and interactive areas.
- Randomised shift objectives.
- Advanced customer AI.
- Full security camera system.
- More complex hiding mechanics.
- Additional equipment and upgrades.
- Daily challenges.
- Employee cosmetics and customisation.
- Story and lore discovery.
- Special multiplayer events.
- More advanced entity AI.
- Additional shift difficulties.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Special boss encounters.
- Randomised entity combinations.

## Out of scope

- Open-world gameplay.
- Player-owned supermarkets.
- Complex economic simulation.
- Competitive PvP.
- Large-scale procedural map generation.
- Excessively graphic horror.
- Complicated hunger, thirst and survival meters.
- Permanent character classes.
- Overly complex crafting systems.

## Development priorities

1. Supermarket environment and interaction system.
2. Core supermarket gameplay mechanics.
3. Shift and objective system.
4. First supernatural entity.
5. Horror events, lighting and audio.
6. Multiplayer and teamwork systems.
7. Progression and rewards.
8. Polish, balancing and testing.

## Design principles

- Supermarket gameplay must be enjoyable independently of the horror.
- Horror should gradually escalate rather than start at maximum intensity.
- Every entity must have recognisable and distinct behaviours.
- Players should be able to learn how to survive encounters.
- Teamwork should be useful but not compulsory.
- Random events should make repeated shifts feel different.
- The game should prioritise atmosphere and tension over cheap jumpscares.
- The first playable version should be achievable without implementing every planned feature.
